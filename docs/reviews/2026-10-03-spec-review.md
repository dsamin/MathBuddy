# Independent review of the MathBuddy proposal

**October 3, 2026 · Reviewed the v0.3 planning packet · Recommendation: revise the specification before asset production. The product and assets are not approved.**

The picnic concept is coherent: a child changes visible quantities, receives gentle feedback and adds something permanent to a small garden. The native iPad requirement is explicit. The proposal also correctly distinguishes a five-year-old family pilot from the much wider eventual ages-four-to-eight curriculum. The main remaining work is to make the content, support rules and production dependencies exact enough that an artist, narrator and native developer cannot interpret them differently.

This is an independent document review, not a review of the experimental app or a learning-efficacy evaluation. No code, assets, builds or Pebble files were changed. References below identify the planning packet as read for this review; later revisions can resolve findings without changing the historical review.

## Preserve

- **One small world and one clear action.** Reusable picnic pieces are a sensible first scope; defer multiple worlds and a generic engine. [Feature spec:47–65](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:47).
- **Visible, reversible mathematics.** Joining conserves the objects; taking away keeps the transferred set available for explanation. Checking the requested transfer separately from the remainder is useful. [Feature spec:136–163](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:136).
- **Honest evidence and equal rewards.** Action-only joining is not independent total recognition, and using help does not reduce the reward. [Feature spec:146–159](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:146), [179–194](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:179).
- **Finite, predictable reward scope.** A pinwheel, five decorations and a freely available garden interaction avoid an unbounded content obligation. Keep the policy visible after the collection is complete. [Feature spec:194–205](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:194).
- **Pebble's review discipline, with MathBuddy's requested sequence.** Stable IDs, retained takes, separate listening approval and native acceptance are worth carrying over. The documents correctly acknowledge that Pebble itself has sometimes worked in parallel, while MathBuddy is to be assets first. [Pebble lessons:7–16](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/research/brightpebble-lessons.md:7).

These are production-readiness findings, not reasons to build another prototype now. Paper compositions, content tables and motion storyboards can settle the proposed contracts within the requested planning stage.

## Resolve in the detailed implementation plan, before coding

1. **Put the minimum settings and continuity contract in the first counting slice.** The later grown-ups phase completes reporting and settings, but counting already needs an entry band, audio controls, motion preference and resume rules. [Roadmap:49–54](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/roadmap.md:49) acknowledges early persistence/accessibility; make the exact Phase 3 subset explicit. This avoids either a hidden debug-only child flow or unnecessary full parent-dashboard work.
2. **Choose the adult gate and its unavailable-authentication behavior.** The accessible alternative remains a placeholder in [feature spec:244](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:244). Define what the family can do on its actual device, including protected reset. This is a design decision to specify, not a reason to add a backend or child arithmetic challenge.
3. **Separate mechanical acceptance from observed usability.** The quantity/reward/persistence checks in [feature spec:298–306](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:298) are appropriate. Add a simple evidence table that separately records automated/Simulator checks, physical-iPad checks, assistive-use review, voice listening and willing child observations. A build or saved-state screenshot cannot close those other rows.
4. **Make early departure predictable across interruptions.** Preserve the current neutral finish-and-resume policy in [feature spec:118](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:118). Specify ownership of an earned-but-unrevealed item and recovery from interrupted help/reward animations in each phase's acceptance examples. The state contract, not the animation callback, must decide whether work is complete.

## Useful later improvements, not pilot blockers

- Keep Discover / Build / Explore as internal planning categories until the small pilot demonstrates its interaction. Do not market the initial three mechanics as full ages-four-to-eight coverage. [Feature spec:82–94](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:82).
- Defer the adaptive 6-of-8 proposal, multi-world map, shared engine and larger reward games. The current adult-selected bands and authored sequences are enough to test the core experience. [Feature spec:181–188](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:181), [269](/Users/devan/Projects/MathBuddy/docs/feature-spec.md:269).
- Review whether five decorations justify their production effort after the direction board. They are optional scope until chosen, not five additional games. Keep the free flower and one satisfying toy as the minimum garden proposition.
- Review larger quantities, comparison and make-five as fresh content batches. In particular, differently spaced sets, empty sets and new object arrangements should be deliberate examples, not unrestricted random layouts.

## Recommended next handoff

The next deliverable is a reconciled specification, exact pilot content/asset ledger and detailed phase plan with explicit dependencies. Record theme, starting band, response modes, finite reward set, voice audition approach and target iPad as decisions, with working recommendations where the user has not yet chosen. Then review the small direction sample, produce the agreed pilot assets in bounded batches, and only afterward select the first native implementation phase.

No production or implementation authorization is inferred from this review. Existing experimental work remains a possible source of candidates, not the design baseline.
